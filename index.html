Foreword

Messages from AUB Leadership

“
Fadlo R. Khuri, MD
President
American University of Beirut

As ESDU marks this 25-year milestone, we honor not only its past achievements but the enduring mission and vision that drives its work.

For twenty-five years, the Environment and Sustainable Development Unit has stood as one of AUB's clearest expressions of purpose: knowledge in service of society. Founded on the belief that development must be rooted in dignity and local agency, ESDU has woven academic rigor with field-based practice to uplift rural communities across Lebanon and the region. Its guiding Motto 'Keepers of the Land' reminds us that sustainability is not a slogan, but a responsibility shared across generations.

What began in 1996 as a small but ambitious set of community-focused initiatives in the Arsal region of Lebanon has grown into a regional leader in applied research and community-driven development. Today, ESDU's work in agroecology, rural livelihoods, food systems, and climate adaptation has produced evidence-based tools and policy insights that continue to inform national and regional programming. Its Master's programs in Rural Community Development and Food Security have become benchmarks for training practitioners, scholars, and policymakers.

The impact of this work is both measurable and inspiring. Over the past 25 years, ESDU has secured more than USD 15 million in competitive grants, implemented 70 projects, strengthened 100,000 livelihoods, and empowered more than 12,000 women as true Keepers of the Land. Its contributions have garnered international recognition, including twelve major awards, while producing 170 publications that have advanced knowledge and practice across multiple fields.

These achievements take on even greater meaning when viewed against the backdrop of Lebanon's recent crises. In times of war, economic collapse, the Beirut port explosion, and multiple displacement waves, ESDU has consistently stepped forward to support cooperatives, municipalities, and small-scale producers. Its rapid interventions have helped restore agricultural activity, stabilize fragile food systems, and reinforce community resilience when national capacities were under unprecedented strain.

ESDU's leadership has also transcended national borders. Through strategic partnerships with institutions such as the Arab Fund for Economic and Social Development and through its regional platform, Karianet, the Unit has stimulated knowledge exchange and strengthened development practice across the Arab region.

“
Ammar Olabi, PhD
Dean, Faculty of Agricultural and Food Sciences
American University of Beirut

Looking ahead, ESDU's work in digital innovation, climate adaptation, and systems-based thinking will remain central to the Faculty's strategic priorities. The next chapter will require agility across disciplines, wider regional connectivity, and sustained commitment to evidence-driven solutions that respond to evolving food system challenges.

Twenty-five years after its establishment, the Environment and Sustainable Development Unit stands as a cornerstone within the Faculty of Agricultural and Food Sciences and as one of its defining pillars. Its evolution reflects more than programmatic growth; it reflects the steady progress of a Faculty committed to shaping sustainable food systems and resilient rural economies.

From a Faculty perspective, ESDU has sharpened our academic leadership. It has firmly embedded the connection between research and field practice, ensuring that our work consistently addresses real-world challenges while upholding the highest standards of scientific rigor. Through this approach, our graduate programs have gained greater clarity, stronger professional alignment, and closer ties to the communities and institutions they serve.

ESDU has also expanded the Faculty's strategic footprint. By building partnerships across Lebanon and the region, engaging actively with municipalities and cooperatives, and contributing substantively to policy dialogue, it has further reinforced FAFS's leadership in agroecology, climate resilience, rural livelihoods, and sustainable food systems. Its efforts have broadened our networks and enhanced the Faculty's regional presence.

Equally important is the academic culture it has helped nurture; one that values applied research, mentorship, and engagement as integral components of excellence. Our students and young professionals benefit from exposure to field realities, collaborative problem-solving, and the kind of grounded experience that shapes responsible leadership. In this way, ESDU has not only implemented projects; it has contributed to the formation of a new generation of practitioners and scholars.

“
Dr. Shadi Hamadeh
Director, ESDU and Professor at FAFS
American University of Beirut

“We woke up from the lethargy produced by the Lebanese war... We wanted to replace death with life, sadness with joy, and despair with hope.”

These words from the Cactus Flower capture not only a moment in time, but the very essence of what would later become the Environment and Sustainable Development Unit.

Twenty-five years ago, a group of young scientists and academics came together carrying more questions than answers. In many ways, they were rebels with a cause; frustrated with systems disconnected from the realities surrounding them and searching for meaning, relevance, and impact. Yet what united them was a shared conviction that knowledge must serve people and that research must remain rooted in the land, in communities, and in real human needs.

From this conviction emerged an ambitious and perhaps idealistic motto: Keepers of the Land.

At the time, it was little more than a dream, a symbolic declaration made by young academics determined to reconnect science with life itself. Soon after, however, the harsh realities of post-civil war Lebanon took over. Daily struggles, institutional limitations, financial constraints, and security concerns consumed both the country and the university environment. The original promise seemed buried beneath layers of urgency and survival.

Yet even as the motto faded into the background, its spirit never disappeared.

The turning point came with the Unit's first project, supported by the International Development Research Centre. It was more than funding; it was validation. It marked the beginning of a journey where research would no longer be confined to academic outputs but would evolve into a tool for transformation and action.

From those early days, the Environment and Sustainable Development Unit (ESDU) grew as a grassroots initiative at heart: resilient, adaptive, and deeply connected to communities. While its impact expanded nationally and regionally, its resources remained modest, often operating at the edge, like Sisyphus pushing the rock uphill time and again. Each effort demanded renewal; each achievement required starting over, yet the climb never ceased.

With the support of visionary leadership, including President John Waterbury and Dean Emeritus Dr. Nuhad Dagher, the Unit was given the space to grow. It gradually became a home for projects that did not fit within traditional academic boundaries: interdisciplinary, community-driven, and solution-oriented initiatives capable of bridging science with society.

Then came the storms that would redefine the meaning of Keepers of the Land.

From economic collapse to the COVID-19 pandemic, from political instability to repeated national crises, Lebanon entered one of the darkest periods in its modern history. International trade froze, livelihoods disappeared, and food insecurity became an immediate reality. Across villages, towns, and even urban balconies, people returned instinctively to the land, searching for dignity, sustenance, and survival.

At that moment, the old motto resurfaced with renewed meaning.

The Keepers of the Land were no longer a small group within a university. They became farmers, mothers, youth, cooperatives, municipalities, researchers, entrepreneurs, displaced families, and entire communities refusing to surrender despair. The land itself became both refuge and resistance.

ESDU answered that calling not as an observer, but as an active participant. The Unit worked where it mattered most: in fragile contexts, underserved regions, and under difficult conditions. It navigated the complex interface between research and development, between knowledge generation and real-world application. It bridged development and relief in moments of crisis, proving that sustainability is not a luxury reserved for stability, but a necessity for survival itself.

Over the years, this mission translated into concrete milestones. From establishing regional knowledge platforms such as Karianet, to launching rural development networks, advancing food security initiatives, promoting the WEFE Nexus approach, supporting vulnerable communities, and creating innovative sustainability models such as the Urban Oasis, the Unit continuously pushed boundaries while remaining grounded in people and place.

Its work received recognition through prestigious national and regional awards, including the PRIMA WEFE Nexus Award, as well as acknowledgments from institutions such as the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations and the University of Guelph.

Winning the Khalifa International Award for Date Palm and Agricultural Innovation not once, but twice, stands as a milestone in itself. It reflects not only excellence in innovation, but consistency in impact and a long-standing commitment to advancing sustainable solutions for the region.

Looking back, this is a story of resilience: not only institutional resilience, but personal and collective resilience. It is a story of persistence in the face of uncertainty, belief in the face of doubt, and action in the face of limitation.

But perhaps above all, it is a story about identity.

Because the Keepers of the Land were never simply a slogan. They became a philosophy, a community, and a shared responsibility. A reminder that despite crises, displacement, and fragmentation, people remain connected to their commons, to nature, to culture, and to one another.

And today, after twenty-five years, the call continues.

Not as the voice of a single unit, but as a growing movement of keepers across Lebanon, the region, and beyond: individuals and communities who believe that science must serve humanity, that sustainability must be lived and practiced, and that meaningful change begins not with abundant resources, but with purpose, courage, and the willingness to act.

Messages from Previous Executive Officers

“
Sarah Karam
Former ESDU Executive Officer
American University of Beirut

Seven years at ESDU left a mark on me that I carry into everything I do.

I joined as a student and grew into a role I hadn't imagined for myself, shaped at every step by colleagues who brought both rigor and genuine heart to their work. What makes ESDU exceptional isn't just the quality of its research and projects — it is the way the team holds together a deep commitment to Lebanon's land and food systems with an equally deep commitment to the people who depend on them.

What I witnessed, and was privileged to be part of, was a unit and a family that took seriously its responsibility to rural communities — not as beneficiaries looked down upon and supported from above, but as true keepers of the land, with knowledge and stakes of their own. Watching applied research connect with the most urgent questions of food and land, pushing toward sustained livelihoods, women's empowerment, and the resilience of food systems — that was a constant reminder of why this work matters. And beyond its reach into communities, ESDU has nurtured generations of students and young professionals into researchers, practitioners, and changemakers.

Twenty-five years is a milestone worth celebrating. Through everything ESDU and this country have endured, the team never gave up on its mission and values — if anything, these challenges made the unit more community-rooted and a stronger advocate for localism. To the team carrying ESDU forward: you are building on something deeply rooted and quietly extraordinary. I am proud to have been part of it.

“
Mabelle Chedid
Former ESDU Executive Officer
American University of Beirut

My tenure as Executive Officer at the Environment and Sustainable Development Unit (ESDU) at AUB, from October 2012 to June 2021, was a defining period in my professional and academic journey.

Working closely with Dr. Shady Hamadeh profoundly shaped my understanding of rural development and local food systems. His mentorship strengthened my analytical skills and deepened my commitment to community-centered, evidence-based development.

During these years, I led and designed research and development projects, developed technical proposals, and oversaw project implementation with a dedicated team of research assistants. I also had the privilege of mentoring students through their final projects and master's theses, an experience that enriched my work and reinforced the importance of nurturing the next generation of researchers and practitioners. In 2013, I co-founded the Food Heritage Foundation with two colleagues, a challenging yet transformative milestone that, with perseverance and Dr. Hamadeh's support, grew into an organization that continues to empower small farmers, food producers, and local food traditions across rural and urban communities.

My work at ESDU accompanied my pursuit of a PhD in France, where my research remained rooted in the projects and communities I served. This dual path strengthened both my academic rigor and my practical understanding of sustainable development. I remain deeply grateful for the opportunities, challenges, and lessons that shaped me during this journey. ESDU continues to safeguard the land and its keepers, and I will always remain an ESDU ambassador, carrying forward its mission and values.

Advancing circular economy, AI, sustainable design, food systems, and heritage through cutting-edge research and innovation.

Keepers of the Land

A Keeper of the Land is anyone dedicated to preserving and protecting our heritage, agriculture, and natural resources for future generations. ESDU's flagship initiative connects small producers to markets, supports land stewards with resources and recognition, and sustains rural livelihoods through the Keepers of the Land Research Fund launched in 2015.
